# Result — personal lines renewal, 9 percent increase

Do not mail this version. Two of the fixes are ten minutes of work; the third is
a question for the agency that no amount of rewriting answers.

## Evidence

The letter is accurate and its reason is good. It loses on ORDER: seven of
thirteen seats stopped reading at or near the premium line, and everything that
would have changed their mind sits below that point. The reshop sentence, which
was the most valued line in the letter among seats who reached it, is in
paragraph three.

The single highest-value sentence in the letter is the one telling the
recent-claim client that the increase is not related to his claim. It held that
account by itself.

## Dissent

The compliance-and-claims reviewer FLAGGED two statements that go over the
agency's signature to a whole cohort: that the increase is unrelated to claim
activity, and that this is the strongest option available at current coverage.
In a mail-merge, both may be false for some recipients.

This puts the compliance seat directly against the recent-claim client, whose
account is held by the first of those sentences. Both are right. Softening the
sentence to make it universally safe would remove the only thing that worked;
the answer is to split the mailing so it goes only where it is true.

The hard-no rejected at intensity 5 for a reason nothing could address, and the
wrong-fit seat was correctly unmoved. Neither is a target for the rewrite.

## Limitations

Thirteen composites built from the agency's stated book and its own recollection
of past mailings. No real policyholder was asked. Staff recollection is recorded
as recollection, not as a count. The panel cannot know the drop date, what else
lands the same week, or what the carrier mails separately. This is a read of a
letter, not a forecast of retention.

## Next experiment

1. **Answer the compliance question first**, because it decides the mailing
   shape: does this cohort include accounts whose renewal DOES reflect claim
   activity, and was the reshop done per account or as a spot check?
2. **Move the reason and the reshop above the number.** Lead with the statewide
   filing and the fact that coverage was compared before the letter was sent,
   then give the number.
3. **Give the dollar figure alongside the percentage**, with last term's amount
   next to it. Three seats asked for this independently.
4. **Have the producer sign it in his own words.** Four seats read the letter as
   institutional; this agency is not.

Then run `rewrite and retest` and compare against this run.

**The real test is the mailing.** Count inbound calls that reference the letter
over 45 days, and note how many were the kind the office wanted. Bring that back
and say "calibrate the panel" — that is what turns this from an opinion into
something worth trusting next time.
