# Synthesis — personal lines renewal, 9 percent increase

**Nine of thirteen seats read this letter as a price-increase notice first and
an agency communication second.** The four who did not are the recent-claim
client, who found the one sentence written for him; the office manager, who
routed it correctly; the compliance reviewer, who is not a customer; and the
hard-no, who was leaving regardless.

The letter is not badly written. It is accurate, the reason is stated, and the
reshop is mentioned. It loses on ORDER: everything that would change a reader's
mind sits below the number, and most of this book does not read that far.

## Agreement clusters

**The number arrives before the reason (7 seats).** The restaurant owner stopped
at the premium line entirely. The price shopper compared the number to the quote
in hand and never reached the reshop sentence. The small commercial operator
deferred. In each case the material that would have changed the decision was
present in the letter and below the point where the reader stopped.

**The reshop is the strongest asset and is buried (5 seats).** "We reviewed your
coverage and compared it against the other carriers we represent" was the single
most valued sentence among seats who reached it, and the loyalist said outright
that he did not know they did that. It is in paragraph three.

**The letter reads institutional (4 seats).** The loyalist's phrase, "it reads
like it came from a building," was echoed by the accountant and the office
manager. This is a family agency writing like a national carrier.

## Consequential dissent

**The compliance reviewer's FLAG caps this result regardless of score.** Two
statements go out over the agency's signature to a whole cohort: that the
increase is unrelated to claim activity, and that this is the strongest option
available. Both may be false for some recipients in a mail-merge. This is not a
copy problem and cannot be fixed by rewriting.

**The recent-claim client is carried by a sentence the compliance seat wants
removed.** This is the run's real tension and neither seat is wrong. The
resolution is a split mailing — the no-claim-relation sentence is true and
valuable for accounts where it is true — not a softened sentence that helps
nobody.

**The hard-no said no, at intensity 5, and gave the correct reason.** Panel
integrity holds. The wrong-fit seat stayed unmoved. No rewrite should target
either.

## Limitations

Thirteen composites, constructed from this agency's stated book profile and its
own recollection of past mailings. No real policyholder was consulted, and the
seat reactions are directional rather than measured. The agency's evidence for
"the phones light up when there is an increase" is staff recollection, not a
count, and the dossiers say so.

This panel cannot know the drop date, what else arrives in the same week, or
what the carrier mails separately. It reads a letter; it does not forecast the
book. The mailing is what tests it.
