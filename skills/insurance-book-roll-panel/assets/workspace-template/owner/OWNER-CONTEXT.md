# Agency Context

This file feeds synthesis and rewriting only. Panel seats never receive it — a
policyholder does not know what the agency is hoping for, and a seat that has
been told the desired answer stops being evidence.

## The agency

Agency name, where it sits, how long it has been there, and who signs the letter.

## What this letter is asking for

What the policyholder is being asked to understand, believe, or do. Be exact:
"call before the renewal date" is different from "do not shop us" is different
from "send your homeowners declarations page."

## What is true and provable

What the agency can actually support in writing — the carrier's filed rate
change, the reshop that was already done, the claims history on the account, the
coverage that actually changed. Every claim the rewrites make comes from here.

## What must not be claimed

Statements the agency will not put in writing: guarantees about future pricing,
comparisons to a named competitor, characterizations of a carrier's motives,
anything the state filing or the carrier agreement constrains, and any promise
about a claim outcome.

## Voice

How this agency actually sounds in writing. A family agency that has been in the
same town since 1963 does not write like a national brand, and a letter that
suddenly does reads as though it came from somewhere else. Note tone, the
signer's habits, and phrases to avoid.

## What the office can absorb

If the letter drives calls, who answers them and how many can be handled in a
week. A letter that generates four hundred calls into a three-person office is a
different problem than a letter nobody answers, and the rewrites should know
which risk matters here.

## The next step

The smallest acceptable action for a client who is interested but not ready.
